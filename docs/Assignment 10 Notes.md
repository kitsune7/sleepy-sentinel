# Assignment 10 — Portfolio Evidence, Risk, and Deployment Draft

## Part A — Problem summary and Portfolio Evidence And Decision Ledger

### Problem, use case, stakeholders, current strategy

- **Problem:** personal early-fatigue aid. Given a short live-video window, estimate whether the user appears **alert, low-vigilant, or drowsy** (ordinal `y ∈ {0,1,2}`).
- **Intended use case:** a self-monitoring awareness aid during long work sessions. Decision support only — the user reads the output and decides. Explicitly **not** safety-critical, **not** client-facing, and **not** for monitoring other people.
- **Affected stakeholders:** (1) the single self-monitoring user (me / a future me) who acts on the output; (2) any person on camera whose face data is processed — a privacy stakeholder even in single-user use; (3) hypothetical third parties if the aid were ever misused to profile someone else (out of charter scope, but a real misuse surface).
- **Current recommended modeling strategy (adopted A8):** `trend_logistic` — frozen pretrained MediaPipe landmarks → per-window geometric summaries → 28 causal cross-window trend features → multinomial logistic regression. Scored at video level after window→video probability averaging, evaluated leave-one-subject-out (60 folds).
- **Scope boundary (unchanged since A4):** no raw-pixel / end-to-end CNN; geometric features from the pretrained landmarker are the representation.

### Decision and operational addendum

**Preliminary decision.** Limit deployment to personal, single-user decision support. The output must remain advisory, low-vigilant messaging must be hedged, and the warm-up limitation must be disclosed. Rows 1–3 and 8 support limited use, while rows 4–7, 9, and 10 prevent broader or autonomous use. Carry this bounded judgment into Assignment 11 without broadening the use case.

**Human oversight and refusal condition.** The user should be able to proactively check a status of the average classification over some set time period and make decisions based on that. But the model should refuse to take any actions that could be relied on in a safety-critical setting.

**Monitoring and reevaluation.** Use periodic, consented, labeled reevaluation to measure per-class recall, drowsy→alert corners, confidence-discrimination gaps, and warm-up usage. Retain only consented labels, predictions, timestamps, and aggregate metrics—never video or face crops. Reevaluate after a material user or setup change or after sufficient labeled evidence shows metric degradation.

**Accountability and deployment constraint.** The single self-monitoring user owns review, trigger response, and enforcement of the privacy constraints. The binding limitation is that evaluation uses acted, constant-label data from a skewed 60-subject cohort without demographic validation or real-use ground truth. Deployment must therefore remain personal and single-user, and a new user or materially changed capture setup requires re-validation.

## Part B — Responsible AI risk and transformer-relevance assessment

### Most consequential responsible-AI risks (each tied to evidence)

1. **Overconfident errors on the early-warning class (miscommunication of certainty).** Tied to ledger rows 4 and 10 — the observed mean confidence-discrimination gap is −0.036 for predictions labeled low-vigilant, and neither temperature scaling nor the tested linear CORN head reverses it. This is not a formal calibration-error metric, but it directly shows that confidence fails to separate correct from incorrect low-vigilant outputs. A confidence-thresholded warning could therefore act confidently on mistakes. This blocks autonomous warning behavior under the tested approaches without claiming that every possible method has been disproved.
2. **Silent drowsy misses (lack of a safety margin).** Tied to row 6 — 11/180 drowsy-called-alert. The aid can say nothing exactly when fatigue is highest. This is why the ceiling is decision support, never a control input.
3. **Silent context-dependence (hidden usage precondition).** Tied to row 5 — the advertised gain requires starting near baseline, a condition the user was never told about. Risk is that a user trusts full-strength behavior during the measurably-weaker warm-up.
4. **Representativeness / unquantified subgroup disparity.** Tied to row 7 — genuinely *missing* evidence, not a resolved concern. Bounded for one user, unbounded for any broader deployment.
5. **Privacy and misuse of behavioral history.** The same causal trend features that flag *my* fatigue could profile someone else's. Mitigations are charter-level and structural: self-monitoring only, consent of anyone on camera, on-device processing, and no retention or transmission of raw video or face crops. If monitoring is enabled, only consented labels, predictions, timestamps, and aggregate metrics may be retained locally. This is a design constraint, not an observed failure — labeled as such.

No generic ethics list is offered; each risk above points at a specific ledger row or an explicitly missing artifact.

### Transformer / pretrained-model relevance decision

**Verdict: transformer-based methods are not adopted because current evidence does not justify prioritizing them.**

- **Pretrained-model context:** the pipeline already depends on a frozen pretrained MediaPipe landmarker, but this does not imply that the landmarker is a transformer. The decision here concerns adding an attention-based classifier or sequence model after landmark extraction.
- **Sequence models: one relevant alternative was tested and rejected.** The A8 GRU lost on every metric and overfit by epoch 6 at 150 training videos (row 2). Together with the weaker MLP, that result shows that the tested higher-capacity alternatives did not pay off at this data scale; it does not prove that every attention architecture would fail.
- **Why a language/pretrained transformer does not fit the problem:** the input is geometric landmark time series, not text or raw pixels; there is no natural-language surface, so LLM hallucination/prompt-injection risks are not in scope, and a vision transformer would reopen the raw-pixel scope boundary the charter closed in A4.
- **The risk a transformer could add here:** at n=60 subjects, a higher-capacity attention model could overfit and invite attention-map overinterpretation—reading meaning into weights fit on only 180 videos—without addressing the observed label, subgroup, and cold-start limitations.
- **What would change this:** materially more representative, real-onset data plus a subject-disjoint experiment showing a specific reliability or performance advantage over `trend_logistic`. That evidence, rather than model novelty alone, would justify reconsideration.

Net: no current evidence shows that a transformer would improve performance, reliability, or deployment risk enough to justify the added capacity. The temporal signal is already represented by causal trend features, while the higher-priority risks are weak middle-class confidence discrimination, silent corner errors, cohort skew, and cold-start dependence.

## Part C — Preliminary deployment judgment

### Judgment: **Limit to decision support** (personal, single-user), under three conditions.

**Intended use and conditions.** Viable as a personal fatigue-awareness aid for the single charter user, under three conditions: (a) output is presented as decision support the user interprets, never a safety-critical warning; (b) the middle ("low-vigilant") band is widened/hedged in any user-facing output; (c) the deployment context states the warm-up requirement — sessions should start near the user's alert baseline, and the first minutes are reduced-accuracy.

**Ledger rows that most support deployment:** rows 1 (beats every floor under a leakage-controlled protocol), 3 (drowsy detection remains stable in the simulated truncation probe), and 8 (a simple luminance-only explanation is not supported). These establish useful signal under the evaluated conditions.

**Ledger rows that block or cap it:** rows 4 and 10 (poor low-vigilant confidence discrimination, not reversed by either tested method) **block** any confidence-gated autonomous warning; row 6 (silent drowsy→alert corners) **caps** the aid at decision support, never a safety control; row 7 (untested subgroups) **caps** it at single-user personal use.

**Why not the other judgments.** *Deploy* is unsupported — low-vigilant confidence does not reliably distinguish correct from incorrect outputs, and subgroups are untested. *Do not use* is too strong — there is useful signal under the evaluated conditions that a self-aware single user can treat as one hedged input to their own judgment. *Gather more evidence* is partly true and is why the judgment is **preliminary**, but enough evidence exists to responsibly permit the narrow decision-support use now while the missing evidence is gathered.

**What new evidence could change the judgment:**
- A separately validated method that produces positive low-vigilant confidence discrimination without sacrificing recall could reconsider the block; temperature scaling and the tested linear CORN head did not meet that bar.
- Per-video KSS scores → would help attribute midpoint misses to label ambiguity versus a missing cue; they would not by themselves prove that the confidence pattern is irreducible.
- Demographic subgroup slices (via local manual annotation) → the only path to any multi-user claim.
- Real mid-fatigue session onsets with ground truth → would confirm or retire the warm-up caveat (row 5 currently rests on simulation).

## Part D — Week 11 operational handoff (draft)

- **Condition requiring human review / refusal to act on output:** every prediction remains advisory and shouldn't trigger a safety-critical action. For a predicted low-vigilant state or during the provisional first minute of a session, the interface should explicitly say that reliability is reduced and ask the user to self-assess rather than assert a state. The one-minute boundary is an operational starting point based on the trend features' approximate history requirement, not a validated safety threshold. This extra messaging cannot catch true low-vigilant cases misclassified as alert or drowsy, which is why the broader refusal of automated action applies to every class.
- **Monitoring signals / metrics (tied to known concerns):**
  1. **Per-class recall, especially low-vigilant recall** (row 4) — estimate this only during periodic reevaluation using sessions for which the user has voluntarily supplied a contemporaneous state label; compare against the documented 21/60 evaluation baseline once enough labeled sessions exist to make the comparison meaningful.
  2. **Drowsy→alert corner rate** (row 6) — on the same labeled sessions, track the safety-relevant silent-failure count.
  3. **Confidence-discrimination gap by predicted class** (rows 4 and 10) — compare mean confidence on correct and incorrect predictions; this requires labels and is distinct from a formal calibration-error metric.
  4. **Warm-up usage** (row 5) — record the fraction of predictions produced during the provisional warm-up period. Whether a session actually started off-baseline is knowable only when the user supplies a starting-state self-report.
- **Monitoring data and privacy:** monitoring is impossible from unlabeled predictions alone. If the user opts in, retain only local predictions, timestamps, voluntary state labels, and aggregate metrics; do not retain or transmit raw video, face crops, or landmarks. Review the accumulated labeled sample periodically rather than interpreting individual sessions as stable rates.
- **Drift / retraining / reevaluation trigger:** re-evaluate when (a) the operating user or capture setup changes materially (new camera, glasses, lighting, or a new user), or (b) a sufficiently sized labeled reevaluation sample shows lower low-vigilant recall or a higher drowsy→alert corner rate than the documented baseline. A new user or material hardware change is an automatic re-validation trigger, not a claim of generalization.
- **Accountable party:** the single self-monitoring user is the accountable reviewer — they own reviewing the consented monitoring summaries, responding to triggers, and enforcing the privacy constraints. There is no separate operator; accountability and use collapse onto the same person by charter design.
- **Most important deployment constraint:** the **data/use-case constraint** — the model is trained and validated only on acted, constant-label UTA-RLDD data from a skewed 60-subject cohort with no demographic columns. This caps honest deployment at personal, single-user decision support and is the root reason multiple rows (5, 7, 9) cannot yet be resolved.

The decision and operational addendum following the Part A ledger, together with this detailed handoff, makes this memo the evidence source for Assignment 11.
